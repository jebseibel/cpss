# Cookbook Business Proposal

Description  
This document lays out a proposal for a new kind of salad book. I have what I think is a revolutionary idea for a salad book but also an awesome name: “Crunch, Punch, Sweet and Savory.”

Background  
I grew up in a household of hamburger helper, white bread and canned soda. A salad meant iceberg lettuce, a few wedges of tomatoes and diced carrots. Most Americans treat salads as a vehicle for salad dressings that we all know are packed with sugar and do very little for your waistline. 

When I became a single dad I realized that I wanted my kids to eat more vegetables than I ever did and it became a struggle (eat your vegetables kids\!).  It got a little easier once they got older but I knew there had to be a way to serve raw veggies in a way they’d LOVE to eat them. That’s when I discovered “crunch, punch, sweet and savory \- the recipe for fantastic salads with or without dressing that are way healthy, easy to make and delicious.

Need  
The marketplace is full of salad books but the most of them are based around leafy greens as a base and then either exotic ingredients or different dressings. They are interesting but let’s be honest, most of us don't have a lot of time and we don’t always have the ingredients handy. My system is different and avoids those problems.

## Crunch, Punch, Sweet and Savory (CPSS) Idea

Here is the formula for CPSS: every salad needs something crunchy, something with punch, something sweet, something savory and a base.Since there are dozens and dozens of items in each of those categories you can create a great salad with whatever you have at hand. Further, with a little extra shopping you can have multiple combinations suited exactly to your families’ taste.

Here is an example recipe:

* Base: Romaine lettuce  
* Crunch: almond slivers  
* Punch: lemon juice  
* Sweet: dried cranberries  
* Savory: feta cheese

Mix with Olive Oil and add salt to taste and it’s an amazing salad everyone will eat. Why will they eat it? 

I’ve had people HATE salads and will eat several helpings of mine. And the best thing is they are eating their veggies.

## What makes this work

I believe that there are several things that make this approach work.

Every bite is different  
You have a lot going on with this that changes how you experience a salad. Traditional salads have crispness with the lettuce, crunch with croutons and taste with the dressing. Instead we put a little of CPSS in so as you eat you get different combinations in your mouth. Think of the previous salad: you get the crunch of almond slivers and maybe some tartness of the lemon and crispness of the lettuce. The next bit might have the savory of the cheese, some tartness of the lemon and sweetness of a small part of dried cranberries. Each bite is different and that’s what it should be. Eat a bowl of corn with butter and that’s all you get. With these salads you get lots of things.

Easy, easy, easy to do  
You can literally look in your refrigerator and pantry and create something and it rarely is the same. Don’t have almonds? Use croutons. Don’t have croutons? Use carrot pieces. Plus you aren’t limited to lettuce. I’ve come up with a few different bases all pretty easy to get. It’s as limited as your imagination.

The Quadrant  
When I first came up with this idea I took a scrap of paper and divided it into four squares: one for each of the CPSS items. Then I wrote some bases under it. It looked like this but more sloppy:

| CRUNCH Almond slivers Carrots \- matchsticks size Celery \- small pieces Broccoli matchsticks size Fresh green bean pieces  Bell pepper pieces Corn   | PUNCH Lemon juice Pepper (ground) Peppercinis Slivers of jalapeno or chilis Green onion pieces Radish Red onion slices Fresh cilantro |
| :---- | :---- |
| SWEET Dried cranberries Dried blueberries Strawberry slices Mini orange slices  | SAVORY Feta cheese Mozzarella cheese cubes Avocado Shaved parmesan  |
| BASE Romaine lettuce Dinosaur Kale lettuce Bell pepper pieces Farro Carrot/celery |  |

Now that you’ve seen the list you can guess that a lot of these items you would never think to combine in a salad, but with care and a delicate hand you can get wonderful combinations. And by the way, the trick is to not let one type overshadow everything else. Too much cheese and it’s bland. Too much punch and it’s too spicy. If you are delicate with each of the quadrants you’ll get great results. Furthermore, we are going to give you measurement guides for each of the ingredients so you don't have to think about that.

## Proposed Book Contents

### Chapter One: Intro of the Idea

Introduction of the idea of CPSS and the quadrant system. We need to have a pull out paper at the back of the book. Discussion of why raw veggies are good for you. Discuss dressings.

### Chapter: Basic combinations for everyone

Basic salad combinations and how to make them. This will give them a springboard into thinking about combinations.

### Chapter: How to Shop

How to use the dry goods sections and save money.

### Chapter: Cutting Veggies and Cheese

General discussion of how to prepare ingredients that work. I’d also like to have a few pages of how to handle certain vegetables because everyone does it incorrectly. There are tricks and tips to make things easy.

### Chapter: Assembling a salad

Discuss that the next chapters are based on ingredients for a four person salad. The idea is that they can take any of the following ingredients and add it for a salad. It’s a mix and match system.

### Chapter: Kids and Salads

Discuss how to get kids to enjoy fresh veggies

### Chapter: Crunch

A short chapter on crunchiness and what it means. Discuss c*runch* items and serving sizes.

### Chapter: Punch

A short chapter on punch and what it means. Discuss punch items and serving sizes.

### Chapter: Sweet

A short chapter on sweet and what it means. Discuss sweet items and serving sizes.

### Chapter: Savory

A short chapter on savory and what it means. Discuss savory items and serving sizes.

### Chapter: Base

A longer chapter on different bases and what it means. Discuss base items and serving sizes.

### Chapter: Dressings

A short chapter on combining olive oil and dressings in unique combinations. Discuss dressing items and serving sizes.

### Chapter: Our Favorite Recipes

The rest of the book will contain our favorite recipes that are easy to make.

## Book or Not to Book

Does this have to be a book? Of course not, but ideas are easy to steal and promote and I think the first to get the idea out there will win in the marketplace of ideas. This could be a YouTube channel, this could be a website but having a book first makes it our original idea.

## Faith in Food

I truly believe that America is ready for something new with vegetables. Healthy eating is becoming mainstream and, more importantly, we need to change how we eat if we are going to kick the obesity cycle. This is best done with new ideas and an active spokesperson.
